Expand description

The gesture-delta camera seam. Every method takes the pose in, answers the resulting pose out, and never mutates: MapCamera is small and Copy, so callers keep single ownership of the live pose while applying deltas.

Pan, focal zoom and rotation are the deltas the reference consumer’s loop produces today; the camera’s own clamps (zoom [0, 24], pitch [0, 75]°, bearing wrapped into [0, 360)) and Mercator latitude bounds always hold on the answer.

fn pan_by_screen_pixels( &self, delta_pixels: [f64; 2], viewport: &ViewportPixels, field_of_view_degrees: f64, ) -> MapCamera

Pans by a pointer travel of delta_pixels (screen pixels, x right / y down): the ground point the pointer just moved over becomes the new camera center, so map content tracks the pointer exactly. Answers the camera unchanged when the delta’s ground ray misses the plane (above the horizon at steep pitch) — the whole-delta rule the reference consumer applies.

Source

fn zoom_by_levels_around_screen_point( &self, delta_zoom_levels: f64, focal_point: [f64; 2], viewport: &ViewportPixels, field_of_view_degrees: f64, ) -> MapCamera

Zooms by delta_zoom_levels (levels; positive zooms in) keeping the world point under focal_point fixed under it. Answers the zoom without the focal correction when the focal ray misses the ground plane (above the horizon at steep pitch). Zoom clamps to the camera’s own level range before the focal correction applies, so a clamped zoom still holds the focal point.

Source

fn rotate_by_degrees_around_screen_point( &self, delta_bearing_degrees: f64, focal_point: [f64; 2], viewport: &ViewportPixels, field_of_view_degrees: f64, ) -> MapCamera

Rotates the bearing by delta_bearing_degrees (degrees clockwise, wrapping into [0, 360)) keeping the world point under focal_point fixed under it. Answers the rotation without the focal correction when the focal ray misses the ground plane — rotating about the focal pixel, the touch-point turn a two-finger gesture implies.
