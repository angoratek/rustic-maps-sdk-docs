The gesture-input camera seam: the delta vocabulary a platform’s gesture recognizer speaks to the SDK (docs/ReferenceAppUX.md §5.1’s owner split — the platform owns gesture recognition, the SDK owns camera math).

The platform loop recognizes gestures (pointer capture, momentum, pinch → deltas) and forwards plain deltas; this seam owns every camera adjustment those deltas imply, reusing the anchored camera’s own clamping and projection math rather than duplicating it. The web playground’s pointer/wheel loop is the reference consumer (M3), and the native platforms wire their Compose/SwiftUI gesture modifiers onto the same methods in M5. What stays platform-side by design: gesture recognition itself, delta shaping (e.g. wheel ticks to zoom levels), inertia after a gesture ends, and any documented zoom-range policy beyond the camera’s own clamp. Pitch has no delta method yet: no consumer drives tilt by gesture (the playground’s plane toggle applies an absolute pitch through the camera object); add the method when a pitch gesture consumer exists.

The focal-point correction is exact, not iterative: a screen pixel’s ground ray direction depends only on bearing, pitch, field of view and viewport — never on zoom or camera position — so after a zoom or rotate delta, translating the camera anchor by the ground offset between where the candidate camera projects the focal pixel and the held ground point puts the world point back under the focal pixel at any pitch.
