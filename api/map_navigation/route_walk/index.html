Distance-parameterised traversal of a planned route’s own geometry (M27).

A simulated trip must follow the polyline the visitor actually planned, not a fixed demo line. RouteWalk flattens a RouteData’s legs → steps → shapes into one traversal-order polyline, collapses duplicate and zero-length segments, accumulates great-circle distances, and answers a fix either at a travelled distance or at an elapsed time. The browser demo and every native shell share this one implementation through the contract; the SDK carries no pacing policy, so the caller supplies a SimulatedTripPace.

§Traversal model

  • Distances between consecutive kept vertices are great-circle haversine distances (the same mathematics as GeographicCoordinate).
  • Vertices closer than [MINIMUM_SEGMENT_LENGTH_METERS] to their predecessor are collapsed, so duplicated step boundaries and repeated shape points contribute no segment and no undefined bearing.
  • A position between two vertices is a linear interpolation in latitude and in antimeridian-unwrapped longitude. This matches how the route polyline is drawn; over road-scale segments the difference from a great-circle arc is far below a meter.
  • Segment bearings are the direction of travel along the active segment, in degrees clockwise from true north (0 = north, 90 = east). At an exact interior vertex the outgoing segment’s bearing wins; the final vertex reports the last segment’s bearing.
  • Longitudes are unwrapped across the antimeridian before interpolation and bearings are computed, then the resulting coordinate is wrapped back into [-180, 180] the way the rest of the SDK normalizes world longitudes. A route from 179.9° to −179.9° therefore traverses 0.2° eastward rather than 359.8° westward.

Both fix functions clamp their argument into the route’s parameter range instead of failing: a negative or NaN distance/time is the trip start, anything past the end is the final fix. SimulatedTripFix::is_arrived is then true exactly when the fix sits at the route’s final coordinate.
