Course filtering (round 11, Ferrostar B7’s mechanism with our own expressions): how a position fix’s reported course becomes the trip’s reported course.

A coarse GNSS fix’s course over ground is often the least trustworthy field the fix carries — a momentary standstill or an urban canyon reads as an arbitrary bearing, while the trip’s own route line is a much stronger statement about the real direction of travel. The reference (stadiamaps/ferrostar) ships this as a controller-level configuration enum, CourseFiltering::{Raw, SnapToRoute}, decided once per trip:

  • CourseFiltering::Raw reports the fix’s course verbatim — the tunnel or canyon exit hatch where a garbage route projection must not override a correct physical course.
  • CourseFiltering::SnapToRoute projects the course onto the route line: while the fix is not judged fully off route, the reported course is the bearing of the route line at the user’s snapped position — every materially disagreeing raw course is replaced in one step, and the course stops jittering against the very line the position was already snapped onto. Without a route line to trust (the fix did not snap, or the projected segment carries no direction), the fix’s own course remains the fall-back, and an unreported course retains the trip’s previous course (the same degradation continuity the snapped position keeps).

The filtering is decided per trip like the step-advance and deviation policies (the StartTrip event carries it), and the reducer stamps every fix’s effective course into [TripNavigationState::course_heading_degrees] during the same position snap pass — so consumers (a course wedge, a follow camera) read one course field instead of re-filtering raw courses per platform. The frozen FFI contract does not carry the field or the policy this round; this stays an internal core seam, and any later exposure is addition-only UDL.
